    Why a gym?

    Everyone agrees listening matters. Almost nobody practices it.

    We get years of training in arguing, presenting and selling — and zero reps in the one skill that decides whether people feel safe with us. Listening is not a personality; it’s a movement pattern. Movement patterns are built with reps. So: reps.

    The three moves

    1
    Reflect — play back the facts in fewer words than they used.

    “So the third time — and to the person you trained.” Proof you were actually in there with them.

    2
    Name — offer one feeling word, gently, as a guess.

    “That sounds humiliating.” Naming an emotion tends to lower its voltage — and if you guess wrong, they get to correct you, which is also being heard.

    3
    Invite — one open question, or simply “tell me more.”

    “What was the moment it hit hardest?” One door opened. Not an interrogation — an invitation.

    The five traps

    Fix-it modeAdvice before understanding. Advice only lands after someone feels heard — and half the time they never needed it.
    Silver lining“At least…” is an eraser rubbed across their feelings. Let the bad thing be bad, together, first.
    Story hijack“I know exactly how you feel — when I…” The spotlight drifted. Borrow your experience only to hand it straight back.
    InterrogationThree questions in a row feels like a cross-examination. One open question is a door; five is a warrant.
    Fast reassurance“You’ll be fine” is often a polite “please stop feeling.” Curiosity first, comfort second.
    The one-breath rule: when they finish, take one full breath before you start. The silence is not a failure — it’s the rep.

    Taking it outside

    One real rep beats ten simulated ones. Tonight, in an ordinary conversation: no advice, no “at least,” no your-turn story. Just reflect, name, invite. Notice how hard your hands itch to fix. That itch is the muscle being found.

    This is a practice tool, not therapy. If someone is in crisis, encourage them to contact local emergency services or a crisis line in their country.
